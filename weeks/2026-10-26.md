## How to read this file

Every `### TASK` block below is one task on the day of its `## ` heading.

- **Type** is one of `shopping`, `make-ahead main`, `make-ahead side`, `main prep`, `side prep`,
  `pre-main`, `pre-side`, `starch`, `side`, `main`.
- Work done before a dish is sorted by two questions: does it keep, and does it take 30 minutes or
  more?
  - **Make-ahead** (keeps, 30 minutes or more, mostly waiting): dahi setting, paneer under a weight,
    yogurt draining. It sits on the day of the first dish that uses it, or earlier when it physically
    needs the lead time, and its **Keeps** line says how many days earlier it could be done instead.
  - **Prep** (keeps, under 30 minutes): a quick sub-recipe that could be done on an earlier day.
  - **Pre-main / pre-side** (doesn't keep, 30 minutes or more): a wait that has to happen just before
    the cooking can start: thawing, marinating, soaking. A dish can have more than one. A pre-main
    belongs to the main only; a side is a dish in its own right, so its waits go in a pre-side.
  - **In the dish** (doesn't keep, under 30 minutes): see the next point.
- Make-ahead and prep tasks are named for the dish that first uses them (`Make-ahead main`,
  `Side prep`). When one sits on an earlier day than that dish, its title starts with the dish's day:
  `Tues make-ahead side`. Later dishes reuse the batch and say so under **Reuses**.
- Fetching something from the room is a line at the top of the dish (**Fetch from the room**), not
  a task.
- Anything that can be done while cooking (toasting and grinding spices, roasting nuts, frying paneer,
  steaming a paste) is in the dish itself, ordered so it overlaps with other steps.
- Spice blends are made fresh inside the dish, in just the amount that dish needs. Nothing is made to
  keep.
- Ingredients are counted once: a make-ahead or prep task owns its ingredients, and the dish that uses
  it lists only the finished item.
- Tasks appear under each day in the order they are done. A main and a side are two separate dishes
  that happen to be made on the same day: neither task mentions the other. Each has its own **Running
  order** field covering only its own tasks (and, for the main, the starch).
- **Mise en place** is the full ingredient list for the task, already measured and grouped by what
  goes into the pan together. The method does not repeat those amounts. Oils are the exception: they
  are named on the Stove line, and the method gives the amount.
- Mise en place containers are listed **largest amount first**, and the containers of spices and
  other jar-system items sit **together at the end**, so the jars are visited in one go.
- Ground spices are **ground fresh from whole**. A container with a `dry grind:` line lists the whole
  spices to grind first, in the cast iron spice grinder. That line always comes first in its
  container and holds nothing else; the `then add:` line under it is what goes into the same
  container unground.
- **Stove** is the pans, pots and pressure cookers the task cooks in, with the lid when one is needed,
  followed by the oils or fats to stand beside it. **Cupboard equipment** is everything else that has
  to be fetched from the cupboard, including small appliances and the Sujata jars. **Appliances** are
  the ones that live on the worktop.

**Quantities.** Every amount inside a task is the **exact cooking amount for 2 portions**. Cook
these. There is no shopping task this week, so there is no buy buffer to confuse them with.

Salt: the book measures coarse salt. With fine table salt use about two-thirds of each amount.

### TASK: Side — Stewed Radishes with Tamarind (#634, x1/2)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** make the radishes
- **Time:** 5 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g radish: 5-6 red radishes, trimmed and halved lengthwise, or daikon, peeled and
  cut into 2.5 cm chunks
- **Spice cup 1:** 3 whole cloves + 1-2 dried red chillies, stems off + 1 cinnamon stick
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/2 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp tamarind concentrate

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the cloves, chillies and
   cinnamon and cook until they sizzle and smell sweet-hot, 10-20 seconds. Immediately add the radish.
2. Lower to medium and stir-fry 1-2 minutes. Cover and simmer, stirring occasionally, until
   fork-tender, 15-20 minutes. The radish releases its own liquid and stews in it.
3. Stir in the sugar, salt and tamarind and simmer uncovered 3-5 minutes. Take out the whole spices
   if you like.

### TASK: Main — Tamilian Okra with Buttermilk-Coconut Sauce (#563, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (thaw the coconut) > start the rice > cook the main
- **Note:** the dahi was made on Mon 19 Oct, so it will be a little sour, which suits a buttermilk
  sauce. If it smells off or shows any mould, do not use it.
- **Reuses:** dahi (made Mon 19 Oct), thinned into buttermilk
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Fetch from the room:** rice flour, 2 tsp
- **Stove:** non-stick pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** slotted spoon, Sujata chutney jar
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Make-ahead (made earlier)**

- 100 g dahi

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1, whisked smooth:** 100 g dahi + 60 ml water (together, the 160 ml buttermilk) + 2 tsp rice
  flour + 1/2 tsp salt + a pinch of turmeric
- **Bowl 2:** 150 g frozen okra. If it is whole pods, take it out 10 minutes before and cut it into
  2.5 cm lengths, then keep it frozen until step 2. Thawed okra goes slimy.
- **Sujata chutney jar:** 40 ml water + the 2 1/2 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 green chilli, stem off
- **Cup 1:** 20 ml water, to rinse the jar
- **Spice cup 1:** 2/3 tsp coriander seeds + a generous pinch of fenugreek seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/3 tsp mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 3:** 4 dried curry leaves

**Method**

1. Heat the non-stick pan over medium-high, add 2 tsp vegetable oil and swirl. Add the coriander and
   fenugreek seeds and stir constantly until reddish brown, about 1 minute. Lift the seeds out with
   the slotted spoon into the jar with the coconut and chilli, leaving the oil behind.
2. Add the mustard seeds to the same oil, cover, and cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds.
   Immediately add the frozen okra (it spatters), keep the heat at medium-high until its water has
   boiled off, then lower to medium and stir-fry uncovered until it blisters and browns lightly,
   12-15 minutes in all.
3. Meanwhile wet grind the coconut, chilli, roasted seeds and water to a slightly gritty paste and
   scrape it into the whisked buttermilk. Swish the rinsing water round the jar, add that too, and
   whisk.
4. In the last minute of browning, crumble the curry leaves into the oil around the okra. Dried
   leaves go into the fat, not the liquid.
5. Pour in the buttermilk-coconut mixture. Stir once or twice and cook uncovered, stirring
   occasionally, until slightly thickened, 3-5 minutes. Serve over the rice.

### TASK: Side — Cabbage and Cucumber Slaw with Roasted Peanuts (#74, x1/4)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** pre-side (thaw the coconut) > make the slaw
- **Time:** 15 min
- **Stove:** tadka pan, and something to cover it; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** Sujata chutney jar
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1 (the serving bowl):** 200 g cabbage, thinly shredded + 1/4 cucumber, peeled, halved
  lengthwise, seeds scooped out, thinly sliced + the 2 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 tbsp chopped coriander
  + 3/4 tsp sugar + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** juice of 1/2 lime
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 tbsp raw peanuts
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/4 tsp mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** a pinch of asafoetida

**Method**

1. Heat the dry tadka pan over medium-high, add the peanuts and cook, stirring now and then, until
   golden in patches, 3-4 minutes. Tip onto a plate to cool.
2. Meanwhile toss the cabbage, cucumber, coconut, coriander, sugar and salt.
3. Heat 1 1/2 tsp vegetable oil in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover, and
   cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds. Off the heat, sprinkle in the asafoetida. Scrape it
   all over the cabbage.
4. Pulse the cooled peanuts to coarse crumbs in the Sujata chutney jar and add them.
5. Add the lime juice and toss well. Leave at room temperature.

### TASK: Main — Garlicky Green Lentils (#422, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** start the rice > cook the main
- **Makes:** about 2 cups
- **Time:** 30 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid, tadka pan; ghee

**Mise en place**

- **Cup 1:** 360 ml water
- **Bowl 1:** 100 g moong dal (skinned split)
- **Small bowl 1:** 3 large cloves garlic, finely chopped + 2 green chillies, thinly sliced, seeds in
  + 1/8 tsp asafoetida
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/8 tsp turmeric
- **Spice cup 3:** 3/4 tsp salt

**Method**

1. Put the dal in the stainless steel pot, half fill with water and rub the dal between your
   fingertips; drain. Repeat three or four times until the water stays fairly clear.
2. Add the measured water and bring to the boil, uncovered, over medium. Skim off the foam and stir
   in the turmeric. Lower to medium-low, cover, and simmer, stirring occasionally, until tender,
   18-20 minutes.
3. While the dal cooks, heat 1 tbsp ghee in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the cumin seeds and
   cook until they sizzle and turn reddish brown, 5-10 seconds. Add the garlic, chillies and
   asafoetida and stir-fry until the garlic is light brown, 1-2 minutes. Take off the heat.
4. Scrape the tempering into the dal, cover, and simmer about 5 minutes. Stir in the salt and serve
   over the rice.

## Thurs 29 Oct

### TASK: Starch — Chapati, from scratch

- **Type:** starch
- **Makes:** 6
- **Time:** 10 min dough, 20 min rest, 15 min rolling and cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan, used dry; ghee, for brushing (optional)
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, tongs, a tea towel to keep them warm

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g atta + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** 100 ml warm water (you will use 90-100 ml)
- **Small bowl 1:** atta for dusting

**Method**

1. Add the water to the atta and salt a little at a time, mixing with your hand until it comes
   together as a soft dough that is not sticky. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Cover and rest
   20 minutes.
2. Divide into 6 balls. Roll each in the dusting atta and roll out to a thin 15 cm round.
3. Heat the dry stainless steel pan over medium-high. Cook a chapati about 30 seconds until small
   bubbles appear, flip, cook 45 seconds until brown spots form, flip again and press the edges with
   the towel so it puffs. On gas, the last step can be done with tongs directly over the flame for a
   few seconds.
4. Stack in the tea towel, brushing each with a little ghee if you like.

### TASK: Main — Bolly Cauli (potatoes and cauliflower, #519, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** chapati dough, then leave it to rest > cook the main > roll and cook the chapati
  while it simmers
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 40 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 225 g potatoes, peeled and cut into 2.5 cm cubes, in cold water
- **Bowl 2:** 225 g cauliflower, cut into 5 cm florets + 200 g chopped tomatoes (half the tin), with
  their juice
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 large cloves garlic + 10 g ginger, both finely chopped
- **Small bowl 2:** 2 tbsp chopped coriander
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 3/4 tsp coriander seeds + a pinch of cumin seeds + a pinch of black peppercorns
    + 1 clove + the seeds of 1 green cardamom pod + 1/4 bay leaf + 1/2 dried red chilli
  - then add: 3/4 tsp salt + 1/8 tsp turmeric

**Method**

1. Drain the potatoes and pat them dry with a tea towel.
2. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the garlic and ginger
   and stir-fry until light brown, about 1 minute.
3. Lower to medium and add the potatoes and the spice cup. Stir-fry until the spices smell fragrant,
   about 2 minutes.
4. Add the cauliflower and tomatoes and stir once or twice. Cover and simmer over medium-low,
   stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are fork-tender, 30-35 minutes. Roll and cook the
   chapati now.
5. Stir in the coriander and serve with the chapati.

### TASK: Main — Chopped Cabbage with a Yellow Split Pea Sauce (#510, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (soak the dal, 2-4 hours ahead) > grind and steam the paste > chapati
  dough while it steams > cook the cabbage > roll and cook the chapati while it simmers
- **Time:** 15 min prep, 45 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** Sujata chutney jar, a shallow heatproof plate that fits inside the pot, a
  small upturned bowl to stand it on, cast iron spice grinder
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the soaked chana dal and chillies, drained, from the pre-main task
- **Bowl 2:** 180 g cabbage (2 cups), finely chopped
- **Cup 1:** 120 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp chopped coriander
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp asafoetida, for the paste
- **Spice cup 2:**
  - dry grind: 1 small dried red chilli
  - then add: 1/2 tsp salt + 1/8 tsp turmeric + 6-7 dried curry leaves
- **Spice cup 3:** 1 1/2 tsp urad dal (skinned, split)
- **Spice cup 4:** 1/2 tsp mustard seeds

**Method**

1. Wet grind the dal and chillies to a slightly gritty, red-speckled paste, adding 1 tbsp water only
   if the blades will not catch. Fold in the salt and asafoetida for the paste.
2. Rub the plate with a few drops of vegetable oil and spread the paste on it about 1 cm thick. Put
   the upturned bowl in the stainless steel pot with 3 cm of water, bring to the boil, set the plate
   on the bowl, cover, and steam until a knife comes out clean, 20-25 minutes. Make the chapati dough
   while it steams.
3. Lift the plate out and leave the cake to cool. Tip the water out of the pot and wipe it dry.
4. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the pot over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover, and cook until
   the popping stops, about 30 seconds. Add the urad dal and stir-fry until golden brown, 15-20 seconds.
5. Add the cabbage and stir-fry to coat it in the oil, about 2 minutes. Sprinkle in the ground chilli,
   salt, turmeric and curry leaves, stir once or twice, and pour in the water. Take off the heat while
   you crumble the cake into pea-size crumbs.
6. Stir in the crumbs, cover, and cook over medium-low, stirring occasionally, until most of the
   liquid has been absorbed into the swollen crumbs, 12-15 minutes. Roll and cook the chapati now.
7. Stir in the coriander and serve with the chapati.
