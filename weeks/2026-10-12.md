## How to read this file

Every `### TASK` block below is one task on the day of its `## ` heading.

- **Type** is one of `shopping`, `main prep`, `side prep`, `pre-side`, `pre-main`, `starch`, `side`,
  `main`.
- A **main prep** or **side prep** task is a sub-recipe the book assumes you already have, named for
  the dish that first uses it. When it sits on an earlier day than that dish for timing reasons, its
  title starts with the dish's day: `Wed side prep`, `Thurs main prep`. It is scheduled on the day of the
  first dish that uses it (or earlier only when it physically needs the lead time). Later dishes reuse
  that batch and say so under **Reuses** — they get no second task. Any prep task can be done on an
  earlier day if there is time.
- Ingredients are counted once: a prep task owns its ingredients, and the dish that uses it lists only
  the finished prep item.
- **Pre-main** and **pre-side** tasks are different from prep tasks. Prep is a sub-recipe that keeps,
  so it can be made a day or more ahead. A pre-main is the work that has to be done ahead of cooking but only makes
  sense shortly before it, on the same day, because it is sensitive to time or space: thawing, rubbing
  or marinating, washing and drying, roasting something that must cool, fetching from the room. It
  would not be done the day before or that morning. A pre-main belongs to the main only. A side is a
  dish in its own right, so its ahead-of-time work goes in its own **pre-side** task.
- Side and main tasks never contain an "ahead" step or a wait. Anything that would slow the cooking
  down or leave me standing around is in the pre-main or pre-side, so opening a main or a side means
  cooking straight through.
- Tasks appear under each day in the order they are done. A main and a side are two separate dishes
  that happen to be made on the same day: neither task mentions the other. Each has its own **Running
  order** field covering only its own prep, pre-task and (for the main) starch.
- **Mise en place** is the full ingredient list for the task, already measured and grouped by what
  goes into the pan together. The method does not repeat those amounts. Oils are the exception: they
  are named on the Stove line, and the method gives the amount.
- Mise en place containers are listed **largest amount first**, and the containers of spices and
  other jar-system items sit **together at the end**, so the jars are visited in one go.
- Ground spices are **ground fresh from whole**. A container with a `dry grind:` line lists the whole
  spices to grind first, in the cast iron spice grinder. That line always comes first in its
  container and holds nothing else; the `then add:` line under it is what goes into the same
  container unground.
- **Stove** is the pans, pots and pressure cookers the task cooks in, with the lid when one is needed,
  followed by the oils or fats to stand beside it. **Cupboard equipment** is everything else that has
  to be fetched from the cupboard, including small appliances and the Sujata jars. **Appliances** are
  the ones that live on the worktop.

**Two sets of quantities — do not mix them up.**

- Every amount inside a main prep, side prep, pre-side, pre-main, starch, side or main task is the **exact cooking amount for
  2 portions**. Cook these.
- The **Buy** column in the shopping task is the cooking amount **plus 25%, rounded up** to something
  buyable. That extra is a buffer against trimming, spoilage and short packs. It is never added to the
  pot, and the cooking amounts must not be scaled up to match it.

Salt: the book measures coarse salt. With fine table salt use about two-thirds of each amount.

### TASK: Tues side prep — Dahi (homemade yoghurt)

- **Type:** side prep
- **Makes:** about 500 g
- **Used in:** Tues radish raita (240 g), Wed paneer in spinach sauce (55 g, drained to thick yogurt),
  Thurs okra (100 g, as buttermilk)
- **Why today:** needs 8-10 hours to set, so it cannot be made on Tuesday
- **Time:** 15 min active, 8-10 h unattended
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot
- **Cupboard equipment:** a clean lidded container of about 750 ml, a thick towel

**Mise en place**

- **Stainless steel pot:** 500 ml whole milk
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 tbsp live yoghurt from the new pot, out of the fridge so it loses its chill

**Method**

1. Bring the milk just to the boil, then cool until you can hold a finger in it for 10 seconds
   (about 43 C). Too hot kills the culture; too cool and it will not set.
2. Whisk the yoghurt with a little of the warm milk and stir it back into the stainless steel pot.
3. Pour into the container, lid on, wrap it in the towel and put it in the oven with only the oven
   light on. Leave it completely undisturbed for 8-10 hours.
4. Tuesday morning: it should wobble as one mass. Move it to the fridge.

### TASK: Main prep — Maharashtrian garam masala (#109, quarter batch)

- **Type:** main prep
- **Makes:** about 4 tbsp
- **Used in:** Tues mackerel (1 tsp). The rest keeps 2 months in a sealed jar, out of the fridge.
- **Time:** 10 min, plus cooling
- **Stove:** tadka pan, used dry
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp raw peanuts + 1 1/2 tsp sesame seeds + 3/4 tsp coriander seeds
  + 1/4 tsp cumin seeds + 2 dried red chillies, stems off + a pinch of grated nutmeg + 1/2 blade mace
- **Spice cup 1:** 1 tbsp desiccated coconut

**Method**

1. Heat the dry tadka pan over medium-high. Add the peanuts, seeds, chillies, nutmeg and mace and
   toast, shaking the pan every few seconds, until the peanuts and sesame are honey-brown and the
   chillies have blackened slightly, 3-4 minutes. Tip onto a plate.
2. Return the pan to the heat and toast the coconut for about 15 seconds, until almond-brown. Add it
   to the plate.
3. Let everything cool completely, then dry grind to the texture of ground black pepper.

### TASK: Main — Pan-Grilled Mackerel with a Tamarind Sauce (#290, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** main prep (ginger chile paste, garam masala) > pre-main (marinate the mackerel)
  > start the rice > cook the main
- **Time:** 35 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan and lid, non-stick pan; vegetable oil

**Prep recipes (made above)**

- 1 tsp Maharashtrian garam masala
- 1 tbsp ginger chile paste, already on the fish

**Mise en place**

- **Plate 1:** the marinated mackerel, from the pre-main task
- **Bowl 1:** 100 g tomato (1/2 large), cored and finely chopped + 1/2 tsp tamarind concentrate
- **Small bowl 1:** 75 g onion (1/2 medium), finely chopped
- **Small bowl 2:** 2 tbsp chopped coriander
- **Plate 2:** 40 g roasted rava, spread out
- **Spice cup 1:** 1 tsp Maharashtrian garam masala + 1/2 tsp salt

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pan over medium. Add the onion and stir-fry until
   soft and light brown round the edges, about 10 minutes.
2. Stir in the tomato and tamarind, then the garam masala and salt. Cook uncovered, stirring
   occasionally, until the tomato softens but still holds its shape, 5-10 minutes. Stir in the
   coriander, take off the heat and cover.
3. Press each fillet into the rava to coat both sides.
4. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the non-stick pan over medium. Fry the fillets until crisp and brown
   on both sides and just starting to flake inside, 4-8 minutes a side depending on thickness.
5. Serve the fish over the rice with the sauce spooned on top.

## Wed 14 Oct

### TASK: Main prep — Paneer (#333, one-third batch)

- **Type:** main prep
- **Makes:** about 190 g
- **Used in:** Wed paneer in spinach sauce. Keeps 1 week in the fridge, so it can be made on Mon or Tues.
- **Why this early in the day:** it needs 3-5 hours under a weight. Start at least 4 hours before dinner.
- **Time:** 20 min active, 3-5 h pressing
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot
- **Cupboard equipment:** colander, a clean thin cloth, a heavy pan filled with water as the weight

**Mise en place**

- **Stainless steel pot:** 1.3 l whole milk
- **Cup 1:** 2 tbsp lime juice (1 lime), or 4 tsp white vinegar

**Method**

1. Bring the milk to the boil over medium-high, stirring often so it does not catch.
2. Stir in the lime juice and take the pot off the heat. Within 30 seconds the curds separate from
   thin, pale green whey. If the whey is still milky, add another teaspoon of juice.
3. Line the colander with the cloth, set it in the sink and pour everything in. Once it is cool
   enough to touch, fold the cloth over the curds.
4. Sit the weight directly on the wrapped cheese and leave until firm, 3-5 hours.
5. Unwrap and refrigerate.

### TASK: Main — Fried Cheese Cubes with a Spinach Sauce (#344, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** main prep (paneer and thick yogurt, 4 hours ahead) > pre-main (thaw the spinach,
  fry the paneer) > cook the main > cook the parathas
- **Time:** 20 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Prep recipes (made above)**

- 190 g paneer, fried
- 40 g thick yogurt

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the 200 g thawed spinach with its liquid, from the pre-main task
- **Plate 1:** the fried paneer, from the pre-main task
- **Bowl 2:** 70 g tomato (1/3 large), cored and finely chopped
- **Small bowl 1:** 35 g onion (1/3 small) + 10 g ginger, minced together finely with a knife
- **Small bowl 2:** 40 g thick yogurt
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/3 tsp cumin seeds + a generous pinch of fennel seeds + 1 dried red chilli, stem off
- **Spice cup 2:**
  - dry grind: 1 tsp coriander seeds + 1/2 dried red chilli
  - then add: 1/2 tsp salt + a pinch of turmeric

**Method**

1. Heat 2 tsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the cumin, fennel and
   whole chilli and cook until they sizzle and turn reddish brown, 10-15 seconds.
2. Immediately add the minced onion and ginger and stir-fry until light brown round the edges,
   5-7 minutes.
3. Stir in the ground coriander and chilli, salt and turmeric and cook 10-15 seconds. Add the tomato
   and cook uncovered, stirring occasionally, until soft but still chunky, about 3 minutes.
4. Add the spinach with its liquid and stir until hot through and the bottom of the pot is scraped
   clean, 3-4 minutes.
5. Stir in the paneer, cover, and simmer, stirring occasionally, until the cheese is warmed through,
   2-3 minutes.
6. Off the heat, fold in the thick yogurt. It will look slightly curdled; that is expected. Serve with
   the parathas.

### TASK: Main — Tamilian Okra with Buttermilk-Coconut Sauce (#563, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (thaw coconut, cut the okra, fetch rice flour) > start the rice > cook
  the main
- **Reuses:** dahi (made Mon), thinned into buttermilk
- **Time:** 5 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Stove:** non-stick pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** slotted spoon, Sujata chutney jar
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Prep recipes (made earlier)**

- 100 g dahi

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1, whisked smooth:** 100 g dahi + 60 ml water (together, the 160 ml buttermilk) + 2 tsp rice
  flour + 1/2 tsp salt + a pinch of turmeric
- **Bowl 2:** the 150 g cut okra, left in the freezer until step 2
- **Sujata chutney jar:** 40 ml water + the 2 1/2 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 green chilli, stem off
- **Cup 1:** 20 ml water, to rinse the jar
- **Spice cup 1:** 2/3 tsp coriander seeds + a generous pinch of fenugreek seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/3 tsp mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 3:** 4 dried curry leaves

**Method**

1. Heat the non-stick pan over medium-high, add 2 tsp vegetable oil and swirl. Add the coriander and
   fenugreek seeds and stir constantly until reddish brown, about 1 minute. Lift the seeds out with
   the slotted spoon into the jar with the coconut and chilli, leaving the oil behind.
2. Add the mustard seeds to the same oil, cover, and cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds.
   Immediately add the frozen okra (it spatters), keep the heat at medium-high until its water has
   boiled off, then lower to medium and stir-fry uncovered until it blisters and browns lightly,
   12-15 minutes in all.
3. Meanwhile wet grind the coconut, chilli, roasted seeds and water to a slightly gritty paste and
   scrape it into the whisked buttermilk. Swish the rinsing water round the jar, add that too, and
   whisk.
4. In the last minute of browning, crumble the curry leaves into the oil around the okra. Dried
   leaves go into the fat, not the liquid.
5. Pour in the buttermilk-coconut mixture. Stir once or twice and cook uncovered, stirring
   occasionally, until slightly thickened, 3-5 minutes. Serve over the rice.

### TASK: Main — Hard-Cooked Eggs with Coconut Milk (#219, x2/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** main prep (Sri Lankan curry powder) > pre-main (hard-boil the eggs) > start the
  rice > cook the main
- **Time:** 20 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot; vegetable oil

**Prep recipes (made above)**

- 1 1/3 tsp untoasted Sri Lankan curry powder

**Mise en place**

- **Cup 1:** 250 ml coconut milk + 2/3 tsp salt + 1/3 tsp turmeric
- **Bowl 1:** the 4 hard-boiled eggs, peeled, from the pre-main task
- **Bowl 2:** 100 g onion (2/3 cup), finely chopped + 1 green chilli, halved lengthwise, seeds in
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/3 tsp fenugreek seeds + 1 cinnamon stick
- **Spice cup 2:** 1 1/3 tsp untoasted Sri Lankan curry powder

**Method**

1. Heat 4 tsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium. Add the fenugreek seeds and
   cinnamon stick and cook until the fenugreek turns reddish brown and the cinnamon smells fragrant,
   15-30 seconds.
2. Add the onion and chilli and stir-fry until the onion is light brown round the edges, 5-8 minutes.
3. Sprinkle in the curry powder and stir for 30 seconds; the onion stops it burning. Pour in the
   coconut milk with the salt and turmeric.
4. Gently add the eggs and simmer uncovered, basting them often, until the sauce thickens slightly,
   5-10 minutes.
5. Serve over the rice with the sauce spooned over the eggs. Take the cinnamon stick out if you like.

## Sat 17 Oct

### TASK: Main prep — Soak chana dal and chillies

- **Type:** main prep
- **Used in:** Sat cabbage with a yellow split pea sauce
- **Why this early in the day:** needs 2-4 hours. Start at least 4 hours before dinner.
- **Time:** 5 min

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 100 g chana dal + 3 dried red chillies, stems off

**Method**

1. Rinse the dal in three or four changes of water, rubbing it between your fingers, until the water
   runs fairly clear.
2. Cover with warm water, add the chillies, cover the bowl and leave at room temperature 2-4 hours.

### TASK: Pre-main — Grind and steam the dal paste

- **Type:** pre-main
- **For:** Sat cabbage with a yellow split pea sauce
- **When:** about 1 hour before cooking the main
- **Time:** 10 min active, 20-25 min steaming, 15 min cooling
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** Sujata chutney jar, a shallow heatproof plate that fits inside the pot, a
  small upturned bowl to stand it on
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the soaked chana dal and chillies, drained
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp asafoetida

**Method**

1. Wet grind the dal and chillies to a slightly gritty, red-speckled paste, with no added water unless
   the blades will not catch (then 1 tbsp). Fold in the salt and asafoetida.
2. Rub the plate with a few drops of vegetable oil and spread the paste on it about 1 cm thick.
3. Put the upturned bowl in the stainless steel pot with 3 cm of water, bring to the boil, set the
   plate on the bowl, cover, and steam until a knife comes out clean, 20-25 minutes.
4. Lift the plate out and let the cake cool until you can handle it, then crumble it into pea-size
   crumbs.

### TASK: Starch — Chapati, from scratch

- **Type:** starch
- **Makes:** 6
- **Time:** 10 min dough, 20 min rest, 15 min rolling and cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan, used dry; ghee, for brushing (optional)
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, tongs, a tea towel to keep them warm

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g atta + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** 100 ml warm water (you will use 90-100 ml)
- **Small bowl 1:** atta for dusting

**Method**

1. Add the water to the atta and salt a little at a time, mixing with your hand until it comes
   together as a soft dough that is not sticky. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Cover and rest
   20 minutes.
2. Divide into 6 balls. Roll each in the dusting atta and roll out to a thin 15 cm round.
3. Heat the dry stainless steel pan over medium-high. Cook a chapati about 30 seconds until small bubbles
   appear, flip, cook 45 seconds until brown spots form, flip again and press the edges with the
   towel so it puffs. On gas, the last step can be done with tongs directly over the flame for a few
   seconds.
4. Stack in the tea towel, brushing each with a little ghee if you like.

### TASK: Main — Chopped Cabbage with a Yellow Split Pea Sauce (#510, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** main prep (soak the dal, 4 hours ahead) > pre-main (grind and steam the dal
  paste) > chapati dough, then leave it to rest > cook the main > roll and cook the chapati while it
  simmers
- **Time:** 25 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the crumbled dal paste, from the pre-main task
- **Bowl 2:** 180 g cabbage (2 cups), finely chopped
- **Cup 1:** 120 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp chopped coriander
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 1 small dried red chilli
  - then add: 1/2 tsp salt + 1/8 tsp turmeric + 6-7 dried curry leaves
- **Spice cup 2:** 1 1/2 tsp urad dal (skinned, split)
- **Spice cup 3:** 1/2 tsp mustard seeds

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds,
   cover, and cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds. Add the urad dal and stir-fry until
   golden brown, 15-20 seconds.
2. Add the cabbage and stir-fry to coat it in the oil, about 2 minutes. Sprinkle in the ground
   chilli, salt, turmeric and curry leaves, stir once or twice, and pour in the water.
3. Stir in the dal crumbs, cover, and cook over medium-low, stirring occasionally, until most of the
   liquid has been absorbed into the swollen crumbs, 12-15 minutes.
4. Stir in the coriander and serve with the chapati.
