## How to read this file

Every `### TASK` block below is one task on the day of its `## ` heading.

- **Type** is one of `shopping`, `make-ahead main`, `make-ahead side`, `main prep`, `side prep`,
  `pre-main`, `pre-side`, `starch`, `side`, `main`.
- Work done before a dish is sorted by two questions: does it keep, and does it take 30 minutes or
  more?
  - **Make-ahead** (keeps, 30 minutes or more, mostly waiting): dahi setting, paneer under a weight,
    yogurt draining. It sits on the day of the first dish that uses it, or earlier when it physically
    needs the lead time, and its **Keeps** line says how many days earlier it could be done instead.
  - **Prep** (keeps, under 30 minutes): a quick sub-recipe that could be done on an earlier day.
  - **Pre-main / pre-side** (doesn't keep, 30 minutes or more): a wait that has to happen just before
    the cooking can start: thawing, marinating, soaking. A dish can have more than one. A pre-main
    belongs to the main only; a side is a dish in its own right, so its waits go in a pre-side.
  - **In the dish** (doesn't keep, under 30 minutes): see the next point.
- Make-ahead and prep tasks are named for the dish that first uses them (`Make-ahead main`,
  `Side prep`). When one sits on an earlier day than that dish, its title starts with the dish's day:
  `Tues make-ahead side`. Later dishes reuse the batch and say so under **Reuses**.
- Fetching something from the room, ingredients or the Sujata jars, is a line at the top of the dish
  (**Fetch from the room**), not a task.
- Anything that can be done while cooking (toasting and grinding spices, roasting nuts, frying paneer,
  steaming a paste) is in the dish itself, ordered so it overlaps with other steps.
- Spice blends are made fresh inside the dish, in just the amount that dish needs. Nothing is made to
  keep.
- Ingredients are counted once: a make-ahead or prep task owns its ingredients, and the dish that uses
  it lists only the finished item.
- Tasks appear under each day in the order they are done. A main and a side are two separate dishes
  that happen to be made on the same day: neither task mentions the other. Each has its own **Running
  order** field covering only its own tasks (and, for the main, the starch).
- **Mise en place** is the full ingredient list for the task, already measured and grouped by what
  goes into the pan together. The method does not repeat those amounts. Oils are the exception: they
  are named on the Stove line, and the method gives the amount.
- Mise en place containers are listed **largest amount first**, and the containers of spices and
  other jar-system items sit **together at the end**, so the jars are visited in one go.
- Ground spices are **ground fresh from whole**. A container with a `dry grind:` line lists the whole
  spices to grind first, in the cast iron spice grinder. That line always comes first in its
  container and holds nothing else; the `then add:` line under it is what goes into the same
  container unground.
- **Stove** is the pans, pots and pressure cookers the task cooks in, with the lid when one is needed,
  followed by the oils or fats to stand beside it. **Cupboard equipment** is everything else that has
  to be fetched from the cupboard, including small appliances. **Appliances** are
  the ones that live on the worktop.

**Two sets of quantities — do not mix them up.**

- Every amount inside a task other than shopping is the **exact cooking amount for 2 portions**.
  Cook these.
- The amounts in the shopping task are what to **buy**: the cooking amount **plus 25%, rounded up**
  to something buyable. That extra is a buffer against trimming, spoilage and short packs. It is never
  added to the pot, and the cooking amounts must not be scaled up to match it.

Salt: the book measures coarse salt. With fine table salt use about two-thirds of each amount.

**Method**

### TASK: Starch — Chapati, from scratch

- **Type:** starch
- **Makes:** 6
- **Time:** 10 min dough, 20 min rest, 15 min rolling and cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan, used dry; ghee, for brushing (optional)
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, tongs, a tea towel to keep them warm

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g atta + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** 100 ml warm water (you will use 90-100 ml)
- **Small bowl 1:** atta for dusting

**Method**

1. Add the water to the atta and salt a little at a time, mixing with your hand until it comes
   together as a soft dough that is not sticky. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Cover and rest
   20 minutes.
2. Divide into 6 balls. Roll each in the dusting atta and roll out to a thin 15 cm round.
3. Heat the dry stainless steel pan over medium-high. Cook a chapati about 30 seconds until small
   bubbles appear, flip, cook 45 seconds until brown spots form, flip again and press the edges with
   the towel so it puffs. On gas, the last step can be done with tongs directly over the flame for a
   few seconds.
4. Stack in the tea towel, brushing each with a little ghee if you like.

### TASK: Main — Chai Patti Chana (chickpeas simmered in black tea with ginger, #372, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** Wed pre-main (soak the chickpeas, Tues night) > make-ahead main (cook the
  chickpeas) > make the chapati dough and rest it > cook the main > cook the chapatis while it
  simmers
- **Reuses:** the cooked chickpeas (made today)
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 20 min cooking
- **Stove:** tadka pan, stainless steel pot; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** sieve, a mug

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the cooked chickpeas (about 250 g) + 2 tbsp chopped frozen coriander + 3/4 tsp salt +
  1/4 tsp turmeric
- **Cup 1:** 240 ml water, for the tea
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp finely chopped ginger + 1/2 tbsp finely chopped garlic + 2 green chillies,
  thinly sliced (seeds in)
- **Small bowl 2:** juice of 1/2 lime
- **Spice cup 1:** 2 black tea bags, or 1 tbsp loose black tea
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/2 tsp cumin seeds

**Method**

1. Bring the water to a rolling boil in the tadka pan, take it off the heat and add the tea. Steep
   5 minutes until deep reddish brown, then strain it into the mug.
2. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the cumin and cook
   until it sizzles and turns reddish brown, 5-10 seconds. Add the ginger, garlic and chillies and
   stir-fry until the ginger and garlic are light brown, 1-2 minutes.
3. Add the chickpea mixture and stir so every chickpea is coated, about 1 minute. Pour in the tea,
   bring to a boil, then cook uncovered over medium until the sauce thickens slightly, 8-10 minutes.
4. Stir in the lime juice.

## Thurs 19 Nov

### TASK: Pre-main — Marinate the chicken

- **Type:** pre-main
- **For:** Batata Murghi tonight
- **When:** at least 30 minutes before cooking; it can go in the fridge from the morning
- **Time:** 15 min, then at least 30 min in the fridge
- **Fetch from the room:** coconut milk (1 tin), Sujata chutney jar
- **Stove:** tadka pan, used dry
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder, a lidded dish for the chicken
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** about 500 g bone-in chicken thighs (4), skin removed
- **Sujata chutney jar:** 40 ml coconut milk (shake the tin first) + 2 tsp coarsely chopped ginger +
  2 cloves garlic + 2 green chillies, stems off + 2 tsp peanuts + 1/2 tsp salt + 1/8 tsp turmeric
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 1 tsp coriander seeds + seeds from 3 green cardamom pods + 1/2 cinnamon stick, broken

**Method**

1. Wet grind everything in the Sujata chutney jar to a slightly smooth but still gritty paste.
   Scrape it into the lidded dish.
2. Toast the coriander seeds, cardamom seeds and cinnamon in the dry tadka pan over medium-high,
   shaking the pan, until the coriander turns reddish brown, about 30 seconds. Tip onto a plate, cool
   5 minutes, then dry grind fine in the cast iron spice grinder. Stir into the paste.
3. Coat the chicken thoroughly in the marinade, cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.
4. Pour the rest of the coconut milk into a lidded container and put it in the fridge. Tonight's
   curry uses 95 ml of it.

### TASK: Main — Batata Murghi (Maharashtrian coconut chicken with potatoes, #178, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (marinate the chicken) > start the rice > cook the main
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 40 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** slotted spoon, a plate

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the marinated chicken, with all its marinade
- **Bowl 2:** 1 small potato (about 120 g), peeled, cut into 2.5 cm cubes, in cold water
- **Cup 1:** 95 ml coconut milk
- **Small bowl 1:** 1/3 onion, halved and thinly sliced
- **Small bowl 2:** 2 tsp chopped frozen coriander + 5 dried curry leaves

**Method**

1. Drain the potato and pat it dry.
2. Heat 2 tsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pan over medium. Fry the potato, stirring, until
   golden brown, 8-10 minutes. Lift it onto the plate with the slotted spoon.
3. In the same pan, cook the onion until light brown at the edges, 5-8 minutes. Add it to the potato.
4. Lift the chicken out of the marinade (keep the marinade) and lay it in the pan in one layer. Sear
   until golden underneath, 3-5 minutes, turn, and sear the other side, 3-5 minutes.
5. Stir in the potato and onion, the coconut milk, the marinade, the coriander and the curry leaves.
   Lower to medium-low, cover and simmer, turning and basting the chicken now and then, until the
   juices run clear at the thickest part, 15-20 minutes.
6. Skim off any oil floating on top and serve the chicken with the sauce and potatoes over it.

### TASK: Starch — Garlic naan

- **Type:** starch
- **Makes:** 4
- **Time:** 15 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan with a lid, used dry; ghee
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, tongs, pastry brush, a tea towel to keep them warm

**Mise en place**

- **Plate 1:** the risen naan dough
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped + 1 tbsp chopped frozen coriander
- **Small bowl 2:** plain flour for dusting

**Method**

1. Divide the dough into 4. Roll each into a teardrop about 5 mm thick. Scatter over some of the
   garlic and coriander and press it in with the rolling pin.
2. Heat the dry stainless steel pan over high until very hot. Wet your hand and dampen the plain side
   of a naan, then lay it in the pan, garlic side up. Cover and cook 1-2 minutes, until big bubbles
   rise and the underside is spotted brown.
3. Flip and cook 1 minute more. On gas, finish it with tongs over the flame for a few seconds to char
   the bubbles.
4. Brush with ghee and keep warm in the tea towel while you cook the rest.

### TASK: Side — Imli Waale Mooli (radishes stewed with tamarind, #634, x1/2)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** make the radishes
- **Time:** 5 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Stove:** tadka pan and something to cover it; vegetable oil

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g radish: 5-6 red radishes, trimmed and halved lengthwise
- **Spice cup 1:** 3 whole cloves + 1-2 dried red chillies, stems off + 1 cinnamon stick
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/2 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp tamarind concentrate

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the cloves, chillies and
   cinnamon and cook until they sizzle and smell sweet-hot, 10-20 seconds. Immediately add the radish.
2. Lower to medium and stir-fry 1-2 minutes. Cover and simmer, stirring occasionally, until
   fork-tender, 15-20 minutes. The radish releases its own liquid and stews in it.
3. Stir in the sugar, salt and tamarind and simmer uncovered 3-5 minutes. Take out the whole spices
   if you like.

### TASK: Main — Masoor Pyaaz Dal (red lentils with caramel-sweet onions, #441, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (naan dough) > start the dal > brown the onions while the dal simmers >
  cook the naan while the onions finish
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 35 min cooking
- **Fetch from the room:** split masoor dal, 100 g
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid, stainless steel pan and lid; ghee
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder, sieve

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 100 g split masoor dal
- **Cup 1:** 360 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 medium onion (about 120 g), halved and thinly sliced + 2 green chillies, finely
  chopped
- **Small bowl 2:** 1/2 large tomato (about 100 g), finely chopped
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 1/2 tsp cumin seeds + 1/4 tsp cardamom seeds (from about 4 pods) + 6 whole cloves +
    1/4 tsp black peppercorns (or 1/4 tsp ground black pepper, added after grinding)
  - then add: 1/2 tsp salt
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/4 tsp turmeric

**Method**

1. Rinse the masoor in the stainless steel pot, rubbing it between your fingers, 3-4 changes of water
   until it runs fairly clear. Drain, add the water and bring to a boil uncovered over medium. Skim
   off the foam. Lower to medium-low, cover and simmer, stirring now and then, until tender,
   18-20 minutes. Keep it warm, covered, on the lowest heat.
2. Meanwhile dry grind the whole spices and mix in the salt.
3. Heat 2 tbsp ghee in the stainless steel pan over medium. Add the onion and chillies and the spice
   mix and stir once or twice. Cover and cook, stirring now and then, until the onion is caramel-brown
   with a deep purple tint and smells sweet, 20-25 minutes.
4. Stir in the tomato and turmeric and cook uncovered until the tomato softens a little, 2-4 minutes,
   scraping up the browned bits.
5. Tip the onion mixture into the dal, stir once or twice and simmer uncovered 3-5 minutes.

### TASK: Side — Muttaikose Poriyal (Tamil cabbage with coconut and peas, #503, x1/3)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** pre-side (thaw the coconut) > make the poriyal
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 15 min cooking
- **Fetch from the room:** Sujata chutney jar
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 120 g white cabbage, finely shredded + 90 g frozen peas (no need to thaw) + 4 dried
  curry leaves + 3/4 tsp salt
- **Sujata chutney jar:** 80 ml water + the 5 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 green chilli, stem off
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/4 tsp black mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1 tsp skinned split urad dal

**Method**

1. Wet grind the coconut, chilli and water in the Sujata chutney jar to a thin, creamy sauce flecked
   with green.
2. Heat 2 tsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pan over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover
   and cook until they stop popping, about 30 seconds. Add the urad dal and stir-fry until golden
   brown, 15-20 seconds.
3. Immediately add the cabbage mixture and the coconut sauce (swish a splash of water round the jar
   and add it too). Stir, bring to a boil, then lower to medium-low, cover and simmer, stirring now
   and then, until the cabbage is just tender, 8-10 minutes.

### TASK: Main — Lasooni Urad Dal (split urad dal with garlic and lime, #408, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** start the rice > start the dal > fry the onion and garlic while it simmers >
  finish the dal
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 35 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid, tadka pan; ghee

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 100 g skinned split urad dal
- **Cup 1:** 360 ml water
- **Cup 2:** 120 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1/2 small onion, halved and thinly sliced + 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped +
  2 green chillies, cut into 5 mm rings (seeds in)
- **Small bowl 2:** juice of 1/2 lime + 1 tbsp chopped frozen coriander
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/4 tsp turmeric
- **Spice cup 3:** 1 tsp salt

**Method**

1. Rinse the urad dal in the stainless steel pot, rubbing it between your fingers, 3-4 changes of
   water until fairly clear. Drain, add the 360 ml water and bring to a boil uncovered over medium.
   Skim off the foam. Stir in the turmeric, lower to medium-low, cover and simmer, stirring now and
   then, until tender but still holding its shape, 15-20 minutes.
2. Meanwhile heat 1 tbsp ghee in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the cumin and cook until it
   sizzles and turns reddish brown, 5-10 seconds. Add the onion, garlic and chillies and stir-fry
   until the onion is lightly browned, 3-5 minutes. Stir in the 120 ml water and the salt and cook
   about 2 minutes, until the onion softens. Set aside.
3. When the dal is cooked, add the onion mixture and simmer uncovered over medium, stirring now and
   then, until slightly thickened, 8-10 minutes.
4. Stir in the lime juice and scatter over the coriander.

### TASK: Side — Aloo Patta Gobhi (cabbage and potatoes with fennel, #507, x1/3)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** make the aloo patta gobhi
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 120 g white cabbage, finely shredded + 1/3 large tomato (about 70 g), cut into 2.5 cm
  pieces + 1 tbsp chopped frozen coriander + 1/2 tsp salt
- **Bowl 2:** 1 small potato (about 75 g), peeled, cut into 1 cm cubes, in cold water
- **Cup 1:** 40 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp chopped frozen coriander, to finish
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/4 tsp fennel seeds + 1/4 tsp cumin seeds + a pinch of fenugreek seeds + 1 dried
  red chilli, stem off
- **Spice cup 2:**
  - dry grind: 1 tsp coriander seeds

**Method**

1. Drain the potato and pat it dry.
2. Heat 2 tsp vegetable oil in the stainless steel pan over medium-high. Add the fennel, cumin,
   fenugreek and chilli and cook until they sizzle, the chilli blackens and the seeds turn reddish
   brown, 15-20 seconds. Add the potato and the ground coriander and stir-fry until lightly browned,
   3-5 minutes.
3. Stir in the cabbage mixture and the water. Bring to a boil, then lower to medium-low, cover and
   simmer, stirring now and then, until the potato is fork-tender, 15-20 minutes. Mash a few potato
   cubes against the pan if you want the sauce thicker.
4. Scatter over the remaining coriander.

### TASK: Main — Hyderabadi Toor Dal (toor dal with tomatoes and browned onions, #470, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pressure-cook the dal > make the tadka while it cooks > finish the dal > cook
  the parathas
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 30 min cooking (including the pressure dropping)
- **Stove:** 2 l pressure cooker, tadka pan and something to cover it; ghee
- **Cupboard equipment:** fine grater

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 100 g toor dal
- **Cup 1:** 360 ml water
- **Cup 2:** 60 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 small onion (about 70 g), finely chopped + 1 tbsp finely grated ginger +
  2 cloves garlic, finely grated + 6 dried curry leaves
- **Small bowl 2:** 1/2 large tomato (about 100 g), finely chopped
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp black mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 3/4 tsp salt + 1/2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder + 1/4 tsp turmeric

**Method**

1. Rinse the toor dal in the pressure cooker, rubbing it between your fingers, 3-4 changes of water
   until fairly clear. Drain, add the 360 ml water and bring to a boil uncovered over high. Skim off
   the foam. Lock the lid; once at full pressure (first whistle), lower to medium-low and cook
   10 minutes. Take off the heat and let the pressure drop on its own, about 15 minutes.
2. Meanwhile heat 1 tbsp ghee in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover and
   cook until they stop popping, about 30 seconds. Add the onion, ginger, garlic and curry leaves and
   stir-fry until the onion is light brown at the edges, 3-5 minutes.
3. Stir in the tomato and the salt, chilli powder and turmeric. Cook uncovered, stirring, until the
   tomato is soft but still holds its shape, 2-3 minutes. Set aside until the dal is done.
4. Stir the tomato mixture into the dal. Pour the 60 ml water into the tadka pan, scrape up the bits
   and add that too.
5. Simmer uncovered over medium, stirring now and then, 3-5 minutes.
