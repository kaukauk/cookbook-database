### TASK: Sat lunch make-ahead — Bread dough, cold-proofed overnight

- **Type:** make-ahead
- **For:** Sat lunch (baked on Friday)
- **Makes:** 1 loaf, about 830 g of dough, 65% hydration
- **Keeps:** 12 to 20 hours in the fridge once shaped. Bake it on Friday within that window.
- **Note:** Water temperature, for a dough at 25°C in a 22°C kitchen: 3 × 25 − 22 (flour) − 22
  (room) − 2 (friction from hand mixing and folds) = 29°C. The yeast is 0.6% of the flour, which
  gives a bulk of about 2½ to 3 hours at 25°C and a slow overnight proof in the fridge.
- **Time:** 15 min hands-on, spread over about 3½ hours, then overnight in the fridge
- **Fetch from the room:** rice flour, for dusting the banneton
- **Cupboard equipment:** large mixing bowl, digital scale, thermometer, dough scraper, banneton, a
  shower cap or cling film

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 500 g strong white bread flour
- **Cup 1:** 325 g water at 29°C (weigh it)
- **Spice cup 1:** 10 g salt
- **Spice cup 2:** 3 g instant dry yeast (1 level tsp)

**Method**

1. Stir the yeast into the flour, then the salt. Pour in the water and mix by hand until there is no
   dry flour left. The dough should read 24-26°C. Cover.
2. Do 4 sets of stretch and folds, one every 30 minutes, over the first 2 hours.
3. Leave it covered until it is about 75% bigger, domed and bubbly at the sides: about 2½ to 3 hours
   from mixing. Go by the dough, not the clock.
4. Turn it out, pre-shape into a loose round and rest it uncovered for 20 minutes.
5. Dust the banneton with rice flour. Shape the dough tightly and put it in seam-side up.
6. Cover it and put it straight in the fridge.

### TASK: Make-ahead main — Garlic confit and garlic oil

- **Type:** make-ahead main
- **Makes:** about 24 soft cloves and 180 ml garlic oil
- **Used in:** the dip and both pizzas tonight; Friday's hummus. Leftover cloves are good spread on
  Saturday's bread.
- **Keeps:** 4 days in a clean jar **in the fridge**, never at room temperature
- **Time:** 15 min hands-on, 45 min on the lowest heat (run it alongside the onions)
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot; extra virgin olive oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** sieve, tea towel, a clean jar of about 300 ml

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 2 bulbs garlic, cloves separated and peeled, left whole
- **Cup 1:** 250 ml whole milk

**Method**

1. Put the garlic and milk in the stainless steel pot, bring it to a simmer and simmer for 3 minutes.
   The milk takes away the harsh edge.
2. Drain and discard the milk, rinse the garlic, then dry it very thoroughly in the tea towel. Wipe
   the pot dry.
3. Return the garlic to the pot with 200 ml extra virgin olive oil; the cloves should be just
   covered (tilt the pot if needed). Set it on the lowest heat: only tiny bubbles, never frying.
4. Cook for 35-45 minutes, until the cloves squash easily and are pale gold.
5. Cool, then pour the cloves and oil into the jar and refrigerate.

### TASK: Make-ahead main — Caramelised onions

- **Type:** make-ahead main
- **Makes:** about 200 g
- **Used in:** Pizza 1 tonight (half); Friday's hummus topping (half)
- **Keeps:** 5 days in the fridge
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 45-55 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan; extra virgin olive oil, butter

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 3 large onions, halved and thinly sliced
- **Cup 1:** 60 ml water
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp salt

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil and 15 g butter in the stainless steel pan over medium heat.
   Add the onions and salt and cook for 10 minutes, stirring, until soft.
2. Turn the heat to medium-low and cook for 35-45 minutes more, stirring every 5 minutes. When the
   pan starts to catch, add a splash of the water and scrape. Done when deep brown and jammy.
3. Cool and refrigerate.

### TASK: Main — Pizza 1: caramelised onion, mushroom and Parmesan

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** dough balls to room temperature > preheat the oven 45-60 min > cook the
  mushrooms > stretch, top and bake
- **Reuses:** caramelised onions, garlic oil (made today)
- **Time:** 15 min, plus the preheat
- **Fetch from the room:** raw semolina, for the peel or tray
- **Stove:** non-stick pan; garlic oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** pizza stone or steel (or an upside-down heavy baking tray), a peel or flat
  baking tray, baking paper, pastry brush
- **Appliances:** oven

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g chestnut mushrooms, sliced 5 mm
- **Plate 1:** 100 g mozzarella, torn and patted dry on kitchen paper
- **Small bowl 1:** half the caramelised onions (about 100 g)
- **Small bowl 2:** 15 g Parmesan, finely grated
- **Small bowl 3:** 1 green chilli, thinly sliced
- **Small bowl 4:** 6 basil leaves
- **Spice cup 1:** a pinch of salt + a few grinds of black pepper

**Method**

1. Put the stone or tray on the top shelf and heat the oven to its hottest setting for 45-60 minutes.
2. Heat 1 tbsp garlic oil in the non-stick pan over high heat. Fry the mushrooms with the salt for
   6-8 minutes, until browned and dry. Their water would make the pizza soggy.
3. Stretch one dough ball by hand on a floured worktop to about 28 cm, keeping a thicker rim. Lay it
   on baking paper on the semolina-dusted peel or tray.
4. Brush the base with 1 tbsp garlic oil, leaving the rim bare. Scatter over the mozzarella, onions,
   mushrooms, chilli and half the Parmesan.
5. Slide it onto the stone, paper and all, and bake for 6-9 minutes until the rim is browned and
   blistered. For more char, switch to the grill for the last minute.
6. Finish with the rest of the Parmesan, the basil, the black pepper and a drizzle of garlic oil.

- **Small bowl 1:** 30 g soft butter + 8 garlic confit cloves + 1 tbsp chopped parsley + a pinch of

- **Small bowl 2:** 20 g Parmesan, finely grated

### TASK: Sat lunch make-ahead — Bake the bread

- **Type:** make-ahead
- **For:** Sat lunch
- **When:** any time today, straight from the fridge, while it is still within 20 hours of shaping.
  Early afternoon is ideal.
- **Keeps:** 2 days in a bread bag or wrapped in a tea towel
- **Time:** 45-60 min preheat, 45 min baking, 2 hours cooling
- **Cupboard equipment:** your lidded baking pot (or a baking stone and a tray for boiling water),
  baking paper, lame or sharp knife, cooling rack
- **Appliances:** oven

**Method**

1. Put the lidded pot in the oven and heat it to 250°C for 45-60 minutes.
2. Turn the dough out of the banneton onto baking paper and score it.
3. Lower it into the hot pot, lid on, and bake for 20 minutes.
4. Take the lid off, turn the oven down to 230°C and bake for 20-25 minutes more, until deep brown and
   hollow-sounding underneath.
5. Cool on the rack for at least 2 hours. Don't slice it; wrap it whole for Saturday.

### TASK: Sat lunch make-ahead — Caramelised onion and garlic confit hummus

- **Type:** make-ahead
- **For:** Sat lunch
- **Reuses:** the soaked chickpeas, garlic confit and garlic oil, the other half of the caramelised
  onions (made Thursday)
- **Keeps:** 3 days in the fridge; keep it cold on the way (it has garlic confit in it)
- **Note:** Fixes for the off taste last time: the garlic is confit, not raw (raw garlic gets sharp
  overnight); no bicarbonate of soda (it can taste soapy); tahini kept to 1 part per 4 of chickpeas
  and stirred well first (the paste at the bottom of the jar is bitter); chickpeas cooked very soft.
- **Time:** 5 min, 35 min pressure cooking and release, 10 min blending
- **Fetch from the room:** Sujata wet grinding jar
- **Stove:** 5 l pressure cooker with its lid; garlic oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** colander, a lidded tub of about 750 ml
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** the soaked chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- **Cup 1:** 1 litre water, for cooking
- **Cup 2:** 80 g tahini, stirred well in its jar first
- **Cup 3:** 75 ml ice-cold water
- **Cup 4:** 45 ml lemon juice (about 1½ lemons)
- **Small bowl 1:** 6 garlic confit cloves
- **Small bowl 2:** the other half of the caramelised onions + 1 tbsp chopped parsley
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
  - then add: 1 tsp salt
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/4 tsp paprika

**Method**

1. Put the chickpeas and the 1 litre of water in the pressure cooker. Bring it up to the first
   whistle on high, then cook on low for 20 minutes. Let the pressure drop on its own.
2. The chickpeas should squash to nothing between your fingers; if not, give them 5 minutes more.
   Drain them, keeping them warm.
3. Blend the tahini, lemon juice, garlic, cumin and salt in the Sujata wet grinding jar for 30
   seconds, until thick and pale. Add 2 tbsp of the ice-cold water and blend again.
4. Add the warm chickpeas and blend for 2-3 minutes, scraping down and adding the rest of the
   ice-cold water bit by bit, until very smooth and light. Taste for salt and lemon.
5. Spread it in the tub, top with the caramelised onions and parsley, dust with the paprika and pour
   over 2 tbsp garlic oil. Lid on, into the fridge.

### TASK: Make-ahead main — Pasta dough

- **Type:** make-ahead main
- **Makes:** about 300 g egg pasta dough
- **Used in:** tonight's tagliatelle
- **Keeps:** 1 day in the fridge, wrapped (bring it back to room temperature for 30 minutes before
  rolling)
- **Time:** 15 min, then 30 min resting
- **Cupboard equipment:** fork, cling film

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 200 g plain flour
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 large eggs
- **Cup 1:** 2 tbsp water, only if needed

**Method**

1. Make a well in the flour, crack in the eggs and beat them with the fork, drawing in flour from
   the sides until it is too stiff to stir.
2. Knead by hand for 8-10 minutes until smooth and springy. If it won't come together, add the water
   a teaspoon at a time.
3. Wrap it and rest it at room temperature for at least 30 minutes.

### TASK: Main — Tagliatelle with butter and Parmesan

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pasta dough (rested) > put the water on > roll and cut > cook and toss
- **Reuses:** pasta dough (made today)
- **Note:** This is the original Roman "Alfredo": no cream, just butter, lots of Parmesan and
  starchy pasta water, tossed hard into a glossy sauce.
- **Time:** 25 min rolling and cutting, 5 min cooking
- **Fetch from the room:** raw semolina, for dusting
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot with lid, stainless steel pan; butter
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, sharp knife, tongs, fine grater, a mug
- **Appliances:** —

**Mise en place**

- **Plate 1:** the rested pasta dough
- **Bowl 1:** 80 g Parmesan, very finely grated
- **Cup 1:** 2 litres water, for the pasta
- **Small bowl 1:** 2 tbsp chopped parsley + zest of 1/2 lemon
- **Spice cup 1:** 1 tbsp salt, for the pasta water
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/2 tsp black pepper

**Method**

1. Bring the water to the boil in the stainless steel pot, lid on. Add the salt.
2. Meanwhile cut the dough in half. Roll each half on a lightly floured worktop as thin as you can,
   about 1 mm: you should see your hand through it. Dust with semolina and leave for 5 minutes.
3. Loosely roll each sheet up, cut it into 7 mm strips and shake them out. Toss them in semolina and
   make nests.
4. Cook the tagliatelle for 2-3 minutes, until just tender. Meanwhile melt 50 g butter in the
   stainless steel pan over low heat with 4 tbsp of the pasta water.
5. Scoop out a mugful of pasta water, then lift the pasta into the pan with the tongs. Take the pan
   off the heat.
6. Add the Parmesan in three goes, tossing hard and adding splashes of the pasta water, until the
   sauce is glossy and coats every strand. Don't put it back on the heat or the cheese will clump.
7. Toss in the black pepper, parsley and lemon zest and eat straight away.
