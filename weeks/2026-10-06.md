## How to read this file

Every `### TASK` block below is one task on the day of its `## ` heading.

- **Type** is one of `shopping`, `main prep`, `side prep`, `pre-side`, `pre-main`, `starch`, `side`,
  `main`.
- A **main prep** or **side prep** task is a sub-recipe the book assumes you already have, named for
  the dish that first uses it. When it sits on an earlier day than that dish for timing reasons, its
  title starts with the dish's day: `Wed side prep`, `Thurs main prep`. It is scheduled on the day of the
  first dish that uses it (or earlier only when it physically needs the lead time). Later dishes reuse
  that batch and say so under **Reuses** — they get no second task. Any prep task can be done on an
  earlier day if there is time.
- Ingredients are counted once: a prep task owns its ingredients, and the dish that uses it lists only
  the finished prep item.
- **Pre-main** and **pre-side** tasks are different from prep tasks. Prep is a sub-recipe that keeps,
  so it can be made a day or more ahead. A pre-main is the work that has to be done ahead of cooking but only makes
  sense shortly before it, on the same day, because it is sensitive to time or space: thawing, rubbing
  or marinating, washing and drying, roasting something that must cool, fetching from the room. It
  would not be done the day before or that morning. A pre-main belongs to the main only. A side is a
  dish in its own right, so its ahead-of-time work goes in its own **pre-side** task.
- Side and main tasks never contain an "ahead" step or a wait. Anything that would slow the cooking
  down or leave me standing around is in the pre-main or pre-side, so opening a main or a side means
  cooking straight through.
- Tasks appear under each day in the order they are done. A main and a side are two separate dishes
  that happen to be made on the same day: neither task mentions the other. Each has its own **Running
  order** field covering only its own prep, pre-task and (for the main) starch.
- **Mise en place** is the full ingredient list for the task, already measured and grouped by what
  goes into the pan together. The method does not repeat those amounts. Oils are the exception: they
  are named on the Stove line, and the method gives the amount.
- Mise en place containers are listed **largest amount first**, and the containers of spices and
  other jar-system items sit **together at the end**, so the jars are visited in one go.
- Ground spices are **ground fresh from whole**. A container with a `dry grind:` line lists the whole
  spices to grind first, in the cast iron spice grinder. That line always comes first in its
  container and holds nothing else; the `then add:` line under it is what goes into the same
  container unground.
- **Stove** is the pans, pots and pressure cookers the task cooks in, with the lid when one is needed,
  followed by the oils or fats to stand beside it. **Cupboard equipment** is everything else that has
  to be fetched from the cupboard, including small appliances and the Sujata jars. **Appliances** are
  the ones that live on the worktop.

**Two sets of quantities — do not mix them up.**

- Every amount inside a main prep, side prep, pre-side, pre-main, starch, side or main task is the **exact cooking amount for
  2 portions**. Cook these.
- The **Buy** column in the shopping task is the cooking amount **plus 25%, rounded up** to something
  buyable. That extra is a buffer against trimming, spoilage and short packs. It is never added to the
  pot, and the cooking amounts must not be scaled up to match it.

Salt: the book measures coarse salt. With fine table salt use about two-thirds of each amount.

### TASK: Wed side prep — Dahi (homemade yoghurt)

- **Type:** side prep
- **Makes:** about 500 g
- **Used in:** Wed radish raita (240 g), Fri okra (100 g, as buttermilk)
- **Why today:** needs 8-10 hours to set, so it cannot be made on Wednesday
- **Time:** 15 min active, 8-10 h unattended
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot
- **Cupboard equipment:** a clean lidded container of about 750 ml

**Mise en place**

- **Stainless steel pot:** 500 ml whole milk
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp live yoghurt, out of the fridge so it loses its chill

**Method**

1. Bring the milk just to the boil, then cool until you can hold a finger in it for 10 seconds
   (about 43 C).
2. Whisk the yoghurt with a little of the warm milk and stir it back into the stainless steel pot.
3. Pour into the container, lid on, and leave somewhere warm and undisturbed for 8-10 hours — the
   oven with only its light on works.
4. Wednesday morning: move it to the fridge.

### TASK: Side — Radish raita (Chopped Radish with Chile-Spiked Yogurt, #75, x1/2)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** Wed side prep (dahi, made Tue) > make the raita
- **Makes:** about 350 ml — half tonight, half for Thursday
- **Reuses:** dahi (made Tue)
- **Time:** 10 min
- **Stove:** tadka pan, and something to cover it; vegetable oil

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1 (the serving bowl):** 240 g dahi, whisked + 60 g daikon, peeled and finely chopped
  (or 2-3 red radishes) + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 tbsp chopped coriander + 4-5 dried curry leaves
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp mustard seeds

**Method**

1. Heat 1 1/2 tsp vegetable oil in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover, and
   cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds.
2. Off the heat, throw in the coriander and curry leaves (they spatter).
3. Pour the tempering into the dahi and radish and fold in. Fridge until dinner. Keeps 4 days.

### TASK: Main — Salmon with Garlic and Turmeric (#291, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (rub the salmon) > start the rice > cook the salmon
- **Time:** 10 min
- **Stove:** non-stick pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Plate 1:** the rubbed salmon, from the pre-main task
- **Cup 1:**
  - dry grind, coarsely: 1/4 tsp black peppercorns
  - then add: 60 ml coconut milk + 1 green chilli, halved lengthwise, seeds in

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in the non-stick pan over medium. Lay the salmon in rubbed side down and
   sear 2 minutes; turn and sear the other side 2 minutes.
2. Stir the coconut milk, pepper and chilli and pour it over the fish. Lift the fillet and tilt the
   pan so the coconut milk runs underneath and lifts the browned garlic.
3. Lower to medium-low, cover, and braise 3-5 minutes, spooning sauce over, until the flesh just
   starts to flake.
4. Serve over the rice with the sauce poured on. Freeze the rest of the coconut milk.

### TASK: Main prep — Tomato paste

- **Type:** main prep
- **Makes:** 2-3 tbsp
- **Used in:** Thu chickpeas with spinach (1 tbsp). Freeze the rest in 1 tbsp blobs.
- **Time:** 35 min, mostly unattended
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan and lid
- **Cupboard equipment:** a sieve over a bowl

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 300 g tomatoes, roughly chopped + a pinch of salt

**Method**

1. Simmer the tomatoes in the stainless steel pan, covered, 10 minutes until they collapse.
2. Push through the sieve to lose skins and seeds. Return the pulp to the pan.
3. Reduce over medium-low, stirring more often as it thickens, 20-25 minutes, until a spoon dragged

### TASK: Main prep — Fried onion paste (#92, small batch)

- **Type:** main prep

- **Used in:** Thu chickpeas with spinach (1 tbsp). The rest keeps 1 week in the fridge, 2 months

- **Time:** 25 min
- **Stove:** non-stick pan; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** hand blender and a tall cup

**Mise en place**

- **Cup 1:** 2 tbsp water

**Method**

   occasionally and then more often as it cooks down, until caramel-brown, 15-20 minutes. Cool on a

2. Blend the cooled onion with the water to a smooth paste.

### TASK: Main — Chickpeas with Spinach and Mustard Seeds (#375, x1/2)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** chickpeas into the pressure cooker > tomato paste and onion paste side by side
  > ginger paste > start the rice > cook the main
- **Time:** 20 min
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; ghee

**Prep recipes (made above)**

- 165 g cooked chickpeas
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tbsp fried onion paste
- 1 1/2 tsp ginger paste

**Mise en place**

- **Cup 1:** 240 ml water
- **Bowl 1:** 225 g spinach, well rinsed and finely chopped
- **Bowl 2:** 165 g cooked chickpeas + 1 tbsp fried onion paste + 1 tbsp tomato paste
  + 1 tsp sambar powder + 1 tsp salt
- **Small bowl 1:** 1 1/2 tsp ginger paste
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/2 tsp mustard seeds

**Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp ghee in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover, and
   cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds.
2. Lower to medium. Add the ginger paste carefully (it spatters) and stir-fry until light brown,
   30 seconds.
3. Tip in the chickpeas with the onion paste, tomato paste, sambar powder and salt, and stir until
   the chickpeas are coated. Pour in the water and bring to the boil.
4. Add the spinach a handful at a time, stirring each in until wilted. Lower to medium-low, cover,
   and simmer, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens, 8-10 minutes.
5. Serve over the rice.

### TASK: Main — Tamilian Okra with Buttermilk-Coconut Sauce (#563, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** pre-main (thaw coconut, dry okra, fetch rice flour) > start the rice > cook the
  main
- **Reuses:** dahi (made Tue), thinned into buttermilk
- **Time:** 10 min prep, 25 min cooking
- **Stove:** non-stick pan and lid; vegetable oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** slotted spoon, Sujata chutney jar
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Prep recipes (made earlier)**

- 100 g dahi

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1, whisked smooth:** 100 g dahi + 60 ml water (together, the 160 ml buttermilk) + 2 tsp rice
  flour + 1/2 tsp salt + a pinch of turmeric
- **Bowl 2:** the 150 g washed and dried okra, caps trimmed without cutting into the pod, cut into
  2.5 cm lengths
- **Sujata chutney jar:** 40 ml water + the 2 1/2 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 green chilli, stem off
- **Cup 1:** 20 ml water, to rinse the jar
- **Spice cup 1:** 2/3 tsp coriander seeds + a generous pinch of fenugreek seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/3 tsp mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 3:** 4 dried curry leaves

**Method**

1. Heat the non-stick pan over medium-high, add 2 tsp vegetable oil and swirl. Add the coriander and
   fenugreek seeds and stir constantly until reddish brown, about 1 minute. Lift the seeds out with
   the slotted spoon into the jar with the coconut and chilli, leaving the oil behind.
2. Add the mustard seeds to the same oil, cover, and cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds.
   Immediately add the okra, lower to medium, and stir-fry until it blisters and browns lightly on
   its ridges, 12-15 minutes.
3. Meanwhile wet grind the coconut, chilli, roasted seeds and water to a slightly gritty paste and
   scrape it into the whisked buttermilk. Swish the rinsing water round the jar, add that too, and
   whisk.
4. In the last minute of browning, crumble the curry leaves into the oil around the okra. Dried
   leaves go into the fat, not the liquid.
5. Pour in the buttermilk-coconut mixture. Stir once or twice and cook uncovered, stirring
   occasionally, until slightly thickened, 3-5 minutes. Serve over the rice.

## Sat 10 Oct

### TASK: Pre-side — Thaw coconut, roast the peanuts

- **Type:** pre-side
- **For:** Sat cabbage and cucumber slaw
- **When:** before starting the chapati dough
- **Time:** 10 min
- **Stove:** tadka pan, used dry
- **Cupboard equipment:** Sujata chutney jar
- **Appliances:** Sujata mixer grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Small bowl 1:** 2 tbsp frozen grated coconut
- **Small bowl 2:** 2 tbsp raw peanuts

**Method**

1. Leave the coconut out to thaw.
2. Heat the dry tadka pan over medium-high, add the peanuts and cook, stirring now and then, until
   golden in patches, 3-4 minutes. Tip onto a plate and let them cool completely.
3. Pulse the cooled peanuts to coarse crumbs in the Sujata chutney jar and return them to their bowl.

### TASK: Starch — Chapati, from scratch

- **Type:** starch
- **Makes:** 6
- **Time:** 10 min dough, 20 min rest, 15 min rolling and cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pan, used dry; ghee, for brushing (optional)
- **Cupboard equipment:** rolling pin, tongs, a tea towel to keep them warm

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 150 g atta + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** 100 ml warm water (you will use 90-100 ml)
- **Small bowl 1:** atta for dusting

**Method**

1. Add the water to the atta and salt a little at a time, mixing with your hand until it comes
   together as a soft dough that is not sticky. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Cover and rest
   20 minutes.
2. Divide into 6 balls. Roll each in the dusting atta and roll out to a thin 15 cm round.
3. Heat the dry stainless steel pan over medium-high. Cook a chapati about 30 seconds until small
   bubbles appear, flip, cook 45 seconds until brown spots form, flip again and press the edges with
   the towel so it puffs. On gas, the last step can be done with tongs directly over the flame for a
   few seconds.
4. Stack in the tea towel, brushing each with a little ghee if you like.

### TASK: Side — Cabbage and Cucumber Slaw with Roasted Peanuts (#74, x1/4)

- **Type:** side
- **Running order:** pre-side (thaw coconut, roast the peanuts) > make the slaw
- **Time:** 10 min
- **Stove:** tadka pan, and something to cover it; vegetable oil

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1 (the serving bowl):** 200 g cabbage, thinly shredded + 1/4 cucumber, peeled, halved
  lengthwise, seeds scooped out, thinly sliced + the 2 tbsp thawed coconut + 1 tbsp chopped coriander
  + 3/4 tsp sugar + 1/4 tsp salt
- **Cup 1:** juice of 1/2 lime
- **Small bowl 1:** the roasted, crushed peanuts, from the pre-side task
- **Spice cup 1:** 1/4 tsp mustard seeds
- **Spice cup 2:** a pinch of asafoetida

**Method**

1. Toss the cabbage, cucumber, coconut, coriander, sugar and salt, then the peanuts.
2. Heat 1 1/2 tsp vegetable oil in the tadka pan over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover, and
   cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds. Off the heat, sprinkle in the asafoetida. Scrape
   it all over the cabbage.
3. Add the lime juice and toss well. Leave at room temperature.

### TASK: Main — Carrots and Peas with Roasted Chile-Spice Blend (#512, x1/3)

- **Type:** main
- **Running order:** chapati dough, then leave it to rest > start the carrots and peas > roll and cook
  the chapati while the curry simmers
- **Note:** the book calls this a side dish. As a dinner it is light; add eggs or a quick dal for more.
- **Time:** 5 min prep, 20 min cooking
- **Stove:** stainless steel pot and lid; sesame oil
- **Cupboard equipment:** cast iron spice grinder

**Mise en place**

- **Bowl 1:** 90 g frozen peas, measured last and left frozen + 75 g carrot, peeled, thinly sliced
  into coins
- **Cup 1:** 80 ml water
- **Small bowl 1:** 50 g onion (1/2 small), finely chopped
- **Small bowl 2:** 2 tsp chopped coriander
- **Spice cup 1:**
  - dry grind: 1/3 tsp cumin seeds + 1/3 tsp coriander seeds
  - then add: 1/2 tsp salt + a pinch of sambar powder + a pinch of turmeric
- **Spice cup 2:** 1/3 tsp mustard seeds

**Method**

1. Heat 2 tsp sesame oil in the stainless steel pot over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds, cover,
   and cook until the popping stops, about 30 seconds. Immediately add the onion and stir-fry until
   honey-brown, 2-3 minutes.
2. Add the salt and ground spices and stir constantly for 1 minute.
3. Add the carrots and peas and stir to coat. Pour in the water and bring to the boil. Lower to
   medium-low, cover, and simmer, stirring occasionally, until the carrots are fork-tender,
   12-15 minutes.
4. Scatter the coriander over the curry and serve with the chapati.
