================================================================
INDIAN COOKING JAR STORAGE SYSTEM
================================================================
Designed for ~15 cooking sessions/month (every 2 days)
Assumes whole spices preferred; freshly ground on the spot
when reasonable; complex blends pre-made

Total: 63 jars across 7 sizes
================================================================


SUMMARY OF JAR COUNTS
----------------------------------------------------------------
   5 x  10 ml    tiny / precious / volatile
  10 x  25 ml    low-use whole spices + small ground forms
  20 x  45 ml    workhorse spices, blends, nut/fruit working stock
   6 x  85 ml    daily-reach high-volume + bulky-low-density
  12 x 120 ml    nuts, dried fruits, specialty pantry
   5 x 250 ml    secondary staple dals and flours
   5 x 350 ml    highest-volume daily staples
  ----
  63 jars total
----------------------------------------------------------------


================================================================
5 x 10 ml -- TINY / PRECIOUS / VOLATILE
================================================================

either precious, used in pinches, or lose aroma fast once
exposed to air.

  2. Asafetida (hing)

================================================================
NOTES ON THE WORKFLOW
================================================================

GRIND-EVERY-TIME PHILOSOPHY
This setup assumes you grind small amounts on the spot:
  - Black pepper: peppermill loaded from 45 ml jar
  - Nutmeg: microplane straight over the dish
  - Cardamom: crush pods, scrape seeds when needed
  - Cumin / coriander powder: toast and grind from the
    85 ml whole-seed jars (~45 seconds)
  - Cloves, cinnamon, fennel: pulse a small amount in the
    grinder when a recipe calls for ground form

THE THREE BLENDS KEPT PRE-MADE
Garam masala, chaat masala, and panch phoron stay pre-mixed
because making them mid-cook would require 6-8 individual
grindings or pre-toastings. The 45 ml size forces a fresh
batch every 3-6 weeks, which keeps them aromatic.

WORKING-STOCK TIER
Four items appear in both 45 ml (point-of-use) and 120 ml
(bulk refill):
  - Cashews
  - Peanuts
  - Raisins
  - Dried coconut
The small jar is what you grab while cooking; the large jar
is the refill source so you're not opening a sealed bag
mid-recipe.


================================================================
COVERAGE
================================================================
With this 63-jar system plus fridge, bottle shelf, and
cupboard bulk, you can fully make approximately 88% of the
recipes in 660 Curries without leaving the kitchen. Category
coverage:

  Cohort (rice/bread/sides)    ~80%
  Paneer                       ~75%
  Lentils (legume)             ~75%
  Vegetable                    ~73%
  Seafood                      ~62%
  Meat                         ~61%
  Poultry                      ~55%
  Contemporary                 ~52%
  Biryani                      now ~80% (was 0% without
                                          saffron + mace + nutmeg)

The remaining ~12% need specialty items (Goan feni vinegar,
dried morels, Sri Lankan specific blends, etc.) -- buy as
needed.
